## Why?

Markowitz chooses portfolios given expected returns. What expected returns make investors' choices consistent with market clearing?

## Who?

Sharpe (1964), Lintner (1965), and Mossin (1966), with earlier contributions from Treynor.

## How?

Under the standard mean–variance equilibrium assumptions, including common beliefs and a common risk-free borrowing/lending rate:

$$
E[R_i]-R_f=\beta_{iM}\big(E[R_M]-R_f\big),
\qquad
\beta_{iM}=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(R_M)}.
$$

Here $M$ is the aggregate market portfolio. In the regression

$$
R_{i,t}-R_{f,t}=\alpha_i+\beta_{iM}(R_{M,t}-R_{f,t})+\epsilon_{i,t},
$$

the model predicts $\alpha_i=0$: diversifiable residual risk earns no separate premium.

## So what?

Beta becomes a benchmark for required returns, hedging, and performance attribution. Standalone volatility is insufficient; covariance with the market matters.

The true market portfolio includes all risky wealth. Testing with a stock index jointly tests the model and its market proxy.

**Research connection:** APT extends the pricing restriction to multiple systematic exposures.
