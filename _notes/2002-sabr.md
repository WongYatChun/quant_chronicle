## Why?

Can stochastic dynamics yield a fast formula for calibrating and managing a smile?

## Who?

Patrick Hagan, Deep Kumar, Andrew Lesniewski & Diana Woodward. Hagan, Kumar, Lesniewski & Woodward (2002). Managing Smile Risk. *Wilmott*.

## How?

For a forward $F$ under its appropriate forward measure,

$$dF_t=\alpha_tF_t^\beta dW_t^F,\qquad d\alpha_t=\nu\alpha_tdW_t^\alpha,\qquad d\langle W^F,W^\alpha\rangle_t=\rho dt.$$

$\alpha_0$ sets the volatility scale, $\beta$ the level dependence, $\rho$ asymmetry, and $\nu$ volatility of volatility. $\beta=0$ gives a normal backbone and $\beta=1$ a lognormal backbone. An asymptotic approximation maps these parameters to implied volatility:

$$\sigma_{\rm imp}(K,T)\approx f(F_0,K,T;\alpha_0,\beta,\rho,\nu).$$

## So what?

Fast smile calibration made SABR influential for rate options. Fixing beta often stabilizes calibration. Extreme strikes and maturities can challenge the approximation; separate expiry fits need not define one consistent dynamic model.
