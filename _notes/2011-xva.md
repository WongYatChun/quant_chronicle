## Why?

How do counterparty risk, collateral, funding, and capital affect contract value?

## Who?

Multiple contributors; Darrell Duffie, Ming Huang, Christoph Burgard & Mats Kjaer. Duffie & Huang (1996). Swap Rates and Credit Quality. *Journal of Finance*.

## How?

In a unilateral setup with independent exposure and default, deterministic discounting, no collateral, and constant recovery $R$,

$$\boxed{\mathrm{CVA}_0=(1-R)\int_0^T D(0,t)E^Q[(V_t^{\rm clean})^+]d\mathrm{PD}^Q(t).}$$

Then $V_0^{\rm adjusted}=V_0^{\rm clean}-\mathrm{CVA}_0$ under these assumptions.

| Adjustment | Source |
|---|---|
| CVA | Counterparty default |
| DVA | Own default |
| FVA | Funding costs and benefits |
| MVA | Initial-margin funding |
| KVA | Capital-cost attribution |

Collateral and netting change residual exposure. Dependence between adverse exposure and default is wrong-way risk.

## So what?

Valuation depends on contract terms and portfolio netting. Future exposure requires future trade values, often leading to nested computation. Adjustments interact and are not universally additive; funding can make valuation nonlinear. Capital and funding charges also involve institutional pricing choices.
