## Why?

An informed trader’s orders reveal information and worsen their execution price. How aggressively should they trade?

## Who?

Albert Kyle, *Continuous Auctions and Insider Trading* (1985).

## How?

Use the **single-auction Gaussian version**, rather than the full dynamic model:

$$V\sim N(p_0,\sigma_V^2),\qquad u\sim N(0,\sigma_u^2),\qquad V\perp u.$$

The insider knows $V$ and chooses $x$. Dealers observe only aggregate signed flow $y=x+u$ and set $p=E[V\mid y]$.

Conjecture $p=p_0+\lambda(x+u)$. The insider solves:

$$\max_x\;x(V-p_0)-\lambda x^2.$$

Combining the first-order condition with competitive Bayesian pricing gives:

$$
x^*=\frac{\sigma_u}{\sigma_V}(V-p_0),\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\sigma_V}{2\sigma_u}.
$$

## So what?

Noise trading provides camouflage. More noise permits greater informed trading and reduces price sensitivity to signed flow.

This impact reflects inference about value. It is distinct from mechanically consuming displayed liquidity. The single-auction coefficients should not be carried unchanged into dynamic settings.

**Research connections:** Glosten–Milgrom adverse selection, Hasbrouck’s empirical impact measurement, and optimal execution.
