## Why?

Can mean-reverting rates remain nonnegative while retaining affine pricing?

## Who?

John Cox, Jonathan Ingersoll & Stephen Ross. Cox, Ingersoll & Ross (1985). [A Theory of the Term Structure of Interest Rates](https://doi.org/10.2307/1911242).

## How?

Under $Q$,

$$\boxed{dr_t=\kappa(\theta-r_t)dt+\sigma\sqrt{r_t}dW_t.}$$

At zero, diffusion vanishes and positive drift pushes upward. With positive parameters, a nonnegative initial rate remains nonnegative. Starting positive, the Feller condition

$$2\kappa\theta\geq\sigma^2$$

makes zero unattainable. If it fails, zero can be reached without negative rates. Bond prices remain $P(t,T)=A(t,T)e^{-B(t,T)r_t}$.

## So what?

Level-dependent rate volatility and tractability. Nonnegativity is restrictive in negative-rate environments; exact initial-curve fit is not automatic. Heston later uses this square-root process for variance.
