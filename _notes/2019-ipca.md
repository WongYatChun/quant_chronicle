## Why?

Ordinary PCA uses fixed loadings. A company's exposures change as its characteristics change. Can observable information describe that evolution?

## Who?

Kelly, Pruitt, and Su, *Characteristics Are Covariances: A Unified Model of Risk and Return* (2019).

## How?

Let $z_{i,t}\in\mathbb R^L$ contain characteristics and let $f_{t+1}\in\mathbb R^K$ contain latent factors:

$$
r_{i,t+1}=\beta_{i,t}^\top f_{t+1}+\epsilon_{i,t+1},
\qquad \boxed{\beta_{i,t}=\Gamma^\top z_{i,t}}.
$$

The restricted model jointly estimates factors and the characteristic mapping:

$$
\min_{\Gamma,\{f_{t+1}\}}
\sum_t\|r_{t+1}-Z_t\Gamma f_{t+1}\|^2,
$$

subject to identification normalizations, typically by alternating least squares. The model can be extended with a characteristic-dependent intercept to test departures from factor pricing.

Assuming conditionally mean-zero residuals,

$$
\boxed{E_t[r_{i,t+1}]=z_{i,t}^\top\Gamma\lambda_t},
\qquad \lambda_t=E_t[f_{t+1}].
$$

## So what?

$$
\text{Characteristics}\rightarrow\text{Exposures}\rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{Expected returns}\\
\text{Return covariance}
\end{cases}
$$

IPCA shares statistical structure across stocks and allows dynamic exposures without estimating a separate loading path for every company.

Realized factor estimation is not a forecast of factor premia. Predicting returns still requires estimating $\lambda_t$. A successful factor fit also does not, on its own, identify an economic mechanism.

**Research connection:** Direct nonlinear prediction relaxes the bilinear expected-return specification.
