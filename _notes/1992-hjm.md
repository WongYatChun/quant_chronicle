## Why?

Can the entire forward curve be modeled directly without introducing arbitrage?

## Who?

David Heath, Robert Jarrow & Andrew Morton. Heath, Jarrow & Morton (1992). [Bond Pricing and the Term Structure of Interest Rates: A New Methodology for Contingent Claims Valuation](https://doi.org/10.2307/2951595).

## How?

Define

$$P(t,T)=\exp[-\int_t^T f(t,u)du].$$

For fixed maturity $T$, under the money-market measure,

$$df(t,T)=\alpha(t,T)dt+\boldsymbol\sigma(t,T)^\top d\mathbf W_t^Q.$$

No arbitrage imposes

$$\boxed{\alpha(t,T)=\boldsymbol\sigma(t,T)^\top\int_t^T\boldsymbol\sigma(t,u)du.}$$

Once volatility is specified, drift is determined. Constant one-factor forward volatility gives $\alpha=\sigma^2(T-t)$, corresponding to Ho–Lee.

## So what?

A framework encompassing many rate models, with the initial curve as input and multiple curve drivers. General specifications need not have a finite-dimensional Markov state, making computation difficult.
