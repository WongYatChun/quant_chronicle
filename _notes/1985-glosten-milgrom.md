## Why?

Can information asymmetry alone generate a spread?

## Who?

Glosten and Milgrom, Bid, Ask and Transaction Prices in a Specialist Market with Heterogeneously Informed Traders (1985).

## How?

Competitive risk-neutral dealers set $A=E[V\mid\mathrm{buy}]$ and $B=E[V\mid\mathrm{sell}]$. In a symmetric illustration, $V=m\pm d$ with equal prior probabilities. A fraction $\alpha$ knows value; the rest buy or sell with equal probability. Bayes’ rule gives:

$$A=m+\alpha d,\qquad B=m-\alpha d,\qquad s=2\alpha d.$$

## So what?

Dealers lose to informed customers and recover expected losses from uninformed flow. After each trade, beliefs update; the symmetric example describes one initial trading step.
